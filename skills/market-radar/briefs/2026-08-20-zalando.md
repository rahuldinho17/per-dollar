# Zalando's agentic engineering post — what it means for PerDollar
*Source: engineering.zalando.com, 14 Aug 2026, Bartosz Ocytko (Executive Principal Engineer). 250+ engineering teams, 2.5 years.*

## The headline: they publicly asked for your product

The post ends with a list of problems they have not solved, and an invitation:

> "We still have a long list of problems to solve, such as: managing tooling and configuration
> on users' devices, local sandboxing, and **auto-routing across models, incl. open-weight ones
> (users rarely switch models unless nudged by hitting a limit or error).** If you're a
> **non-vendor** engineering team tackling similar problems... **feel free to get in touch.**"

Read what that sentence contains. A European commerce company with 250+ engineering teams is
publicly asking for auto-routing across models including open-weight ones, notes that people
only switch models when something breaks, and specifies **non-vendor** — meaning they do not
want it from someone selling them inference.

That is PerDollar, described by the buyer, with an open door and a named author.

## Six technical takeaways

**1. "Users rarely switch models unless nudged."** Third independent confirmation of Ryan's
thesis. Governance by policy does not work at 250 teams any more than at five; the choice has
to be answered where it is made. Zalando's own diagnosis of why is more precise than ours: the
nudge only arrives as a limit or an error, i.e. too late and for the wrong reason.

**2. They found a user at <30% cache hit rate against an 80% expectation** and had to write a
parser to locate the session. They also auto-inject prompt-caching checkpoints through LiteLLM
hooks "for custom agents while their authors still learn about prompt caching," and built a TUI
that "highlights gaps in usage of cached tokens." Everything in `hosts.html` about caching
changing which host wins is validated by a company operating this at scale.

**3. They run LiteLLM in production** — since January 2024, 2k monthly users on six small pods,
restarted every 20k requests to survive memory leaks. The integration recipe in
`integrations/litellm.md` is not hypothetical for them; it is their actual stack.

**4. Vendor independence is stated policy.** "We have never centrally mandated the use of a
single tool." Users pick tools; the proxy provides optionality. An independent decision layer
fits that philosophy; a vendor router does not.

**5. They already have the data a counterfactual needs** — anonymised per-model cost tracking
via post-call hooks, plus session-level analysis using agentsview and codeburn. They can compute
what a task actually cost. What they cannot compute is what it *should* have cost.

**6. Compliance is operational, not theoretical.** Per-use-case legal assessments, and they
auto-detect AI model usage by scanning deployed Docker images, auto-registering systems and
requiring documentation. A European company at this level of governance discipline is the
residency feature's ideal buyer.

## The pattern worth noticing

Zalando is the **third** organisation found to have built cost measurement internally —
ShopAgentic built cost-per-agent-run, Aries' clients track spend, Zalando has a cost TUI and
anonymised per-model tracking.

All three built the measurement. None built the decision. None has a verified price source.
That is the clearest evidence yet that the moat is the decision layer plus provenance, not the
dashboard — and it is a straight line from Kai telling you the price data was the valuable part.

## Why this shape

He is a principal engineer at a company that just published a lab notebook rather than a
highlight reel, so the register is peer-to-peer and technical. It opens with his own words,
answers the specific thing he asked for, states the non-vendor point he specified, and offers

ugly graphs will respond to.
