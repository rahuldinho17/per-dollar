# Navigara — competitor, and the most useful mirror we have found
*25 Aug 2026*

## What they are

An engineering-analytics platform that grades every commit into a metric they call Engineering
Throughput Value, then builds four products on top: Engineering Performance, Roadmap Alignment,
CapEx/OpEx reporting, and **AI ROI**. Real logos, mostly European — Kiwi.com, ESET, FTMO,
Partners Banka, Purple Technology. A public benchmark index across 676 engineers at Microsoft,
OpenAI, Meta, Google, Vercel and Cloudflare. A white paper and a published methodology.

## The bit that is directly competitive

Buried in their product menu: **"Token Spend Intelligence — Route each task to the cheapest model
that can do it, and price the rest."**

That is PerDollar's sentence, shipped as one feature of a larger platform.

This is now the third time in a month: Ramp Router, Cursor Router, and now Navigara. Routing keeps
showing up as a *feature* of something with a bigger reason to exist. That is a pattern, not a
coincidence, and it is the clearest argument yet that standalone routing does not survive as a
company.

## The bit that is far more useful

**They answer the question we cannot.** PerDollar reports what a decision saved. Navigara reports
what the spend *bought*:

> $124,880 of AI spend across 7 tools → **+56 engineers of capacity** nobody had to hire →
> **$2,230 per AI-added engineer**, held against what hiring the same capacity would cost.

And their reasoning for that framing is better than the framing itself:

> *"A percentage is hard to sign off. Capacity is not."*

That is the difference between a cost tool and a budget conversation. "We cut inference 40%" asks
a CFO to care about a line item. "$124,880 bought 56 engineers you did not have to hire" answers
the question the CFO was already asking. Savings is a smaller idea than capacity.

Three more moves worth taking:

**They split the gain by work category** — features 51.8%, maintenance 22.3%, tests, docs, fixes —
with the sharp observation that *"if those buckets balloon, you're paying the tools to clean up
after the tools."* A cost-per-task number that does not distinguish new value from cleanup is
flattering itself.

**They tie it to the roadmap through Jira** — 48.6% of spend traced to a named objective, 28.7%
to no stated outcome. That is the "so what" that turns a dashboard into a board slide.

**They lead with a public failure, not a promise.** A Fortune headline: *"Uber burned through its
entire 2026 AI budget in four months. Now its COO is questioning whether it's worth it,"* with
the COO quoted saying the link between AI spend and outcomes *"is not there yet."* That is the
strongest public evidence this problem exists, and it is free to cite.

## The warning aimed straight at our metric

> *"Pull requests are the wrong denominator: activity is exactly what inflates when work gets
> cheaper to produce."*

Accepted Work per Dollar counts **tasks**, and tasks are activity. A team that halves its cost per
accepted task by splitting work into more, smaller tasks has improved nothing and will show a
better AWpD. We built the metric two days ago and it already has a known gaming vector, named by
someone who thought about the same problem longer.

This does not kill AWpD — cost per accepted task is still more honest than cost per attempted
task — but it does mean the metric needs a denominator that is not chosen by the person being
measured. Navigara's answer is to grade the *output* (commit content) rather than count events.
Ours could be: report AWpD alongside task-size distribution, so a shrinking average task size is
visible rather than hidden.

## Honest read on the competitive position

**Where they beat us:** an answer a CFO can sign, real customers, published methodology, a public
benchmark as marketing, and routing already included.

**Where we are not competing:** they need deep access — commit history, Jira — which is a long
security review and a trust barrier. PerDollar needs neither; it is a lookup. They have no data
residency, no verified price provenance, and no independent price feed. Their routing feature
almost certainly prices from list data nobody audits.

**The uncomfortable synthesis:** Navigara measures the *output* side of the same equation and we
measure the *input* side. Neither is complete alone. They are more compelling today because the
output side is what gets signed off.

## What I would actually do

**1. Steal the capacity framing immediately.** It costs nothing and it is better. "This model
choice costs you the equivalent of X developer-months a year" beats "you could save $5,652 a
month" in every room where a budget is approved. It also fixes the weakest slide in the board
deck, which currently leads with a spread rather than a benefit.

**2. Cite the Uber story.** It is the public proof that the problem is real, from a company nobody
can dismiss, and it makes the case better than any claim of ours.

**3. Fix the AWpD denominator before anyone uses it.** Report task-size distribution alongside
the metric, so shrinking tasks are visible. Better to ship it with the flaw named than to have a
customer find it.

**4. Consider them a partner, not a rival.** Their Token Spend feature needs exactly what we
have — verified prices, EU coverage, an independent decision — and they have what we cannot get
without a pilot: customers and an output metric. They are a European engineering-analytics company
selling to the same buyer. That is a conversation worth having before it is a competition worth
losing.

**5. Do not build engineering analytics.** Grading commits is their moat, it needs deep access, and
they are two years ahead. The line stays where it was: we are the independent price and decision
layer, and the output side belongs to whoever the customer already trusts with their repository.
