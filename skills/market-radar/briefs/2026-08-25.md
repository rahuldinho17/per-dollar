# PerDollar Market Radar — Week of 25 Aug 2026

## 1. This week in one line

Data residency reportedly carries a flat 10% price uplift on newer models — which means the feature PerDollar leads with is currently priced wrong, showing EU-constrained buyers a number that is too low.

## 2. What happened

### Signal

- **Residency endpoints reportedly cost 10% more.** Spheron's August comparison states that any regional data-residency endpoint on a model released on or after 5 March 2026 adds a flat 10% uplift on top of standard rates. **Why it matters:** PerDollar does not model this anywhere. `residency` is currently a filter, not a price dimension — so a Germany-constrained query returns a cost that may be 10% under the truth, on the exact feature the whole positioning rests on. Needs first-party confirmation before we change numbers, but if it holds it is the most consequential gap in the product. (high, unverified)

- **Claude Sonnet 5 may be $2 input, not $3.** CloudZero reports Anthropic made Sonnet 5's introductory $2 permanent rather than letting it lapse to $3, and places it in a three-way tie at $2 with GPT-5.6 Terra and Gemini 3.1 Pro. **Why it matters:** our ledger says $3/$15, human-verified 13 August. This is precisely the promo-becomes-permanent case the README flags as needing manual re-verification, and it is a 33% error on a mainstream production model if true. (high, needs checking)

- **CloudZero now publishes verified prices with a changelog.** Their August comparison states rates were verified against each provider's own docs on a named date and tracks what shifted and when. **Why it matters:** verified provenance with a change log was our differentiator. A well-funded FinOps company has now adopted it. What they still lack is EU-resident coverage, per-job costing and a decision layer — but "we verify" is no longer a claim only we make. (high)

- **The $2 tier is where the fight is.** Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Terra and Gemini 3.1 Pro all sit at exactly $2 input; CloudZero calls it "the most contested price point in AI." **Why it matters:** at identical input prices, output rate and answer-length factor decide the winner — which is exactly what PerDollar computes and a price table cannot. Good demo material. (medium)

- **CostGoat is at 380+ models**, up from 363 a fortnight ago, with new entrants like Ling-2.6-flash at $0.01/$0.03. **Why it matters:** confirms the coverage decision. Chasing that count was never winnable; the Coverage section on the site now explains why we do not try. (low)

- Assorted "cheapest model" roundups recycling the same tables.

## 6. The thing worth saying plainly

Two of this week's four findings are potential errors in our own data, both on models a human verified twelve days ago, and one of them sits on the feature the entire positioning depends on.

That is not a failure of the verification process — it is the process working, since we caught them by reading the market rather than by a customer telling us. But it is a reminder that a 26-model ledger with human verification still drifts within a fortnight, and the answer is the agent doing more of the checking, not the human doing more.
