# W35 addendum — the suggested changes have to change
*After Simon Taylor's post, 25 Aug 2026*

## What the post contains that the brief missed

**Ramp launched Router.com generally available on the same day Stripe announced the OpenRouter
acquisition.** One API to every major model, and — the phrase that matters — *"each request sent
to the cheapest model that clears your quality bar."* Free through 2026. Switching is a base URL
change. Ramp reports early users cutting inference costs 40% on average.

That sentence is PerDollar's positioning, almost word for word, shipped by a company with seven
years of spend-cutting distribution and no need to charge for it.

Three other things from the same thread:

- **"Whoever owns routing owns model procurement. Every request that passes through one is a
  purchasing decision."** The clearest articulation yet of why this category is being bought.
- **Corporate AI spend is up 20.7× since June 2025** (Ramp's own data). Tokens went from rounding
  error to fastest-growing P&L line in fourteen months.
- A reply in the thread, unprompted: **"The only metric that cannot be gamed is Accepted Work per
  Dollar, and neither Ramp nor OpenRouter govern using that metric."**

That last one is the most valuable sentence anyone has written about this market this year, and it
describes the thing PerDollar built and has never been able to demonstrate.

## What this changes

**W35-03 was aimed at the wrong competitor.** Sharpening the claim against CloudZero is now
beside the point. CloudZero publishes a table. Ramp ships a free proxy with identical positioning
and a distribution machine behind it. The comparison that matters is Ramp Router, and it needs a
different answer than "we verify our prices."

**The "cheapest model that clears your quality bar" line is no longer ours to use.** It is on our
homepage twice and in the playground. Using a competitor's marketing copy as our own positioning
is the weakest possible place to stand, regardless of who wrote it first.

**What actually survives against a free router:**

| | Ramp Router | PerDollar |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Proxy — base URL change, in the request path | Not in the request path; sits beside whatever gateway you have |
| Incentive | Ramp monetises money saved; the savings claim is theirs to make and theirs to measure | Sells no inference, takes no share; the saving is computed from your traffic |
| Measure | 40% cost reduction, vendor-stated, against an unstated baseline | Counterfactual against the model *your* engineer would have picked |
| Governs on | Cost clearing a quality bar | Cost, capability, **jurisdiction**, and budget adherence |
| Residency | None. US fintech routing to US-operated APIs | Germany / EU / EU-preferred, over hosts no aggregator lists |
| Outcomes | Not recorded | `outcome` and `retries` per task; observed pass rates per model per task |

The honest summary: **on routing, Ramp wins on distribution and price and we should stop
competing there.** On jurisdiction and on measured outcomes, they are not competing at all.

## The uncomfortable part

AWpD is the right claim and we cannot currently make it. Fourteen shipped items, zero graded,
zero real tasks routed — so the ledger that would produce an Accepted Work per Dollar figure has
never seen a real task.

Ramp can say "40% average savings" because they have users. We can say "here is a metric that
cannot be gamed, and here is the code that computes it" — which is a weaker claim today and a
stronger one the moment a single team runs it for two weeks.

That makes the pilot the whole game, not one backlog item among many. The Zalando outreach is now
the highest-value action in the project, because Bartosz publicly asked for auto-routing and has
250+ teams generating exactly the outcome data this claim needs.
