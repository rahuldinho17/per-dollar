# PerDollar Product Review — September 2026

*Second monthly review. The first graded nothing because everything was days old. Five weeks
on, most items can now be graded honestly, and the answer is uncomfortable.*

**Revised 2 Sep** after Kai replied. One reply from one real user changed three conclusions —
which is itself the review's finding, arriving in the same week it was written.

## 1. The one thing

Fourteen changes shipped, zero real tasks routed, and three warm technical contacts have gone
quiet. The build loop is working beautifully and the distribution loop has not started. Every
item that graded **worked** improved the product's honesty; not one improved its reach.

## 2. What we heard — field notes

**33 notes, of which 16 are research and 11 are real conversations.** That ratio is the problem
in one line: the roadmap is being driven by reading, not by users.

### Patterns (≥2 sources)
- **Routing is a feature, not a company** (4 sources): Ramp Router, Cursor Router, Navigara's
  Token Spend, and OpenRouter's own CEO saying copycat routers are "playing to exist rather than
  to win." Four independent confirmations is not a signal any more, it is a fact.
- **Everyone builds the measurement, nobody builds the decision** (3 sources): ShopAgentic,
  Zalando and Navigara have each built internal cost tracking. None has a verified price source.
- **The buyer is interested but not urgent** (3 sources): Ryan — "not the biggest problem on our
  plate today." Kai and Zalando silent. No one has said the problem isn't real.

### Anecdote worth acting on
- Navigara's capacity framing — "$124,880 bought 56 engineers you didn't hire" — is a better
  answer than any savings figure we produce.

### What Kai's reply corrected (30 Aug, read 2 Sep)

Three things, and two of them contradict decisions we made confidently:

**The residency taxonomy is the wrong way round.** We built `eu-de` as the headline case and demo
it in every message. Kai: *"primarily if they don't want the US involved for compliance reasons.
So in most cases the EU only claim is already fine. Some are so security focused that they want /
need DE. But that is rare."* The driver is **negative** — exclude US processing — not positive.
EU satisfies most; Germany is the edge case we have been leading with.

**The caching angle does not hold at the cheap end.** We treat the 6.3× cache-driven spread
between hosts as a demo moment. Kai has not looked at cached rates at all: inputs vary too much
for good hit rates, and *"as those are open source model in the pretty cheap area I was too lazy
to care for the cost here."* Caching matters at the frontier, where the absolute numbers are
large. At the budget end nobody bothers, which is exactly where our EU hosts sit.

**Breadth was the wrong instinct on EU hosts.** We carry nine. He uses two — Scaleway and IONOS.
Depth on those two, verified first-party, beats nine tracked ones.

He caveats it himself — *"just our very narrow image of the world here"* — so this is one
informed data point, not a market study. But it is the only real one we have, and it outranks
every competitor teardown in this document.

That is the clearest lesson available: **building the honest thing works, rewriting the pitch
does not.** Positioning cannot be validated without someone to position against.
