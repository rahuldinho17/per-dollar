# PerDollar Product Review — September 2026

*Second monthly review. The first graded nothing because everything was days old. Five weeks
on, most items can now be graded honestly, and the answer is uncomfortable.*

## 1. The one thing

Fourteen changes shipped, zero real tasks routed, and three warm technical contacts have gone
quiet. The build loop is working beautifully and the distribution loop has not started. Every
item that graded **worked** improved the product's honesty; not one improved its reach.

## 2. What we heard — field notes

**33 notes, of which 16 are research and 11 are real conversations.** That ratio is the problem
in one line: the roadmap is being driven by reading, not by users.

### Patterns (≥2 sources)
- **Routing is a feature, not a company** (4 sources): Ramp Router, Cursor Router, Navigara's
  Token Spend, and OpenRouter's own CEO saying copycat routers are "playing to exist rather than
  to win." Four independent confirmations is not a signal any more, it is a fact.
- **Everyone builds the measurement, nobody builds the decision** (3 sources): ShopAgentic,
  Zalando and Navigara have each built internal cost tracking. None has a verified price source.
- **The buyer is interested but not urgent** (3 sources): Ryan — "not the biggest problem on our
  plate today." Kai and Zalando silent. No one has said the problem isn't real.

### Anecdote worth acting on
- Navigara's capacity framing — "$124,880 bought 56 engineers you didn't hire" — is a better
  answer than any savings figure we produce.

Every item that graded *worked* is about **provenance or capability** — the feed, the
verification pass, capability floors, the decision endpoint, field capture. Every item that
graded *no-effect* is **positioning or messaging** — copy rewritten three times for an audience
that never read it.

That is the clearest lesson available: **building the honest thing works, rewriting the pitch
does not.** Positioning cannot be validated without someone to position against.
