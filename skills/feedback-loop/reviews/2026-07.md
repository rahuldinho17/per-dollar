# PerDollar Product Review — July 2026 (first run)

## 1. The one thing

Two funded, technical buyers (ShopAgentic, and the Aries agency channel) have now
independently told you the same thing: the price *data* — verified, multi-vendor,
with history — is the asset they can't build themselves, more than the front-end.
That, plus the a16z "systems compound" lesson, says the product's centre of gravity
should shift from *calculator* to *trusted data feed + the loop that keeps it honest*.

## 2. What we heard (field notes)

### Patterns (≥2 sources)
- **Data trust is the real product** (2 sources): Kai (ShopAgentic) already built the
  calculator and asked where prices come from; the whole first-feedback round attacked
  data credibility ("looks AI-generated", "tasks too vague"). The recurring objection is
  never "I can't do the math" — it's "I don't trust these numbers." *Build implication:*
  every credibility feature (verified-vs-tracked badges, source links, the public feed,
  re-verification) is core, not polish.
- **Buyer = invoice approver, sold via the technical gatekeeper** (2 sources): Ale named
  the VP/CFO as buyer; Aries routes through Ryan, a technical AI lead. *Implication:* the
  landing page speaks to the approver, but the door is opened by an engineer — the hosts
  page and the feed are the engineer-credibility layer that earns the meeting.

### Anecdotes (1 source, watch)
- Multi-vendor OSS hosting matters to agentic architectures specifically (Kai). One
  source, but a sharp one — already shipped as hosts.html; watch whether it recurs.
- Free-form task input requested (Ale). Shipped (custom task). Watch for reuse.

## 3. What the market did (from market-radar, W30)

- Ramp shipped a free LLM router + paid AI-spend dashboard — the "give away infra, sell
  controls" play, from a $44B incumbent. The defensible response is vertical depth +
  verified independent data, not feature parity. (high signal)
- Cost-*performance* (not cost alone) is now the metric the market openly ranks models on
  (Algorithmic Bridge, citing Artificial Analysis). Your methodology explicitly doesn't
  model capability yet — the largest open gap between you and how buyers now think.
